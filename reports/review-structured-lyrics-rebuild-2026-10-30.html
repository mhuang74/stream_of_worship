How to read this

  • Pre-run structured lyrics are unrecoverable. No audit table stores lyric text (log_change records counts only); Neon PITR retention is 6 h while the rebuild started 2026-10-29T06:56:33Z; the live project has only its production branch; no DB dump exists on this host; and re-running the heuristic parser over the current raw text reproduces 0 of the 212 pre-run md5s.
  • “Provable” means byte equality of the stored column. Because storage serialisation is deterministic (json.dumps(structured, ensure_ascii=False)), a changed md5 means the parse itself changed — not just formatting of the JSON.
  • The proxy compares the new parse against ASR text, not against the rebuild’s delta. lyrics.lrc is a transcript whose line breaks are driven by ASR timestamps, so line-level and even text-level differences are expected regardless of what the rebuild changed. lrc_source over this corpus: youtube_transcript 252, null 53, youtube_transcript_asr_tail 34, qwen3_asr 1. A material verdict therefore means “the new parse is textually far from the ASR transcript”, which is often the better outcome, not a regression.
  • Verbatim here can be coincidental. An S0 match means the new parse happens to reproduce the ASR line text and line breaks exactly; it does not prove the rebuild preserved something.
  • Every row has a proxy verdict. All 340 recordings had a readable lyrics.lrc object, so no row is missing from the proxy split.
  • Row count is live. The corpus query returned 340 rows at generation time; nothing is padded to a target count.
